The six sessions 🧭 · from the first call-flow map to go-live

The running case is Callwell, a constructed clinic booking line. Three invented sites, one phone number. Callers speak; some text a photo of a referral letter; they expect an answer about as fast as a person would give one; some interrupt. The text chatbot already books appointments through the same system. Callwell, its sites, clinicians, callers and letter are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

🎙️ Session 1 · foundations

The voice pipeline: cascaded or speech-to-speech

What a phone call adds to the agent loop you already built, the two ways to wire a voice onto it drawn as mechanisms, why streaming decides most of the second, and Callwell's call flow mapped end to end.

▶ Start here ⏱️ Session 2 · bench night

The latency waterfall: where the second goes

Four published answers to fast enough, then the bench: a waterfall from the caller's last word to the first syllable, computed live, with a tool-call break button and a cut-the-wait trap measured on real pauses.

★ the bench 🔁 Session 3 · core

Turn-taking: when to speak, when to stop

Voice activity detection, silence timers against semantic turn detection, barge-in and truncation that keep the context honest, backchannels, and end-of-turn settings tuned on real pauses.

who speaks next 📷 Session 4 · deeper

Images in the loop: reading what callers send

Photos and documents as input: OCR against a vision model, extraction to a schema, confidence and asking back. An invented referral letter, read field by field. No image generation.

vision as input 🧪 Session 5 · deeper

Evals for a conversation you cannot read

Task success first, word error rate computed for real, latency and interruption measures, judging audio against transcripts, and simulated callers.

measure the call 📞 Session 6 · ship it

Shipping the call: phones, handoffs and consent

Telephony at concept level, fallbacks and the warm handoff to a person, recording and AI-voice notices from sourced general rules, cost per minute, and Callwell's go-live checklist.

What is honest here 🧾

Voice AI changes monthly and its numbers travel without their sources. Each claim on these pages carries its status, and every product name, default and price is dated 2026-11-08.

ClaimStatus
False cut-in rates for a silence threshold, pause and gap sharesReally measured in your browser from 24 no-eye-contact dialogues of the HCRC Map Task Corpus (CC BY 4.0 per its download page); silence durations only, no audio, no words
Every latency total on the benchComputed from dated vendor targets and labelled modelled inputs; it measures no product
Word error rates in session 5Real arithmetic on constructed Callwell transcripts
"Humans respond in 200 ms"True as an average, misleading as a target. Stivers et al. (2009): mean +208 ms for answers to yes/no questions in ten languages; people get there by planning while the other person talks
"500 ms is the magic number"; "callers hang up after N seconds"No primary source found on 2026-11-08; attributed as opinion or left out
